# Healthcare Analytics Lab: OLTP to Star Schema


**Author:** David Kamanda
**Database:** MySQL 8 (built and tested in MySQL Workbench)


Data engineering mini project (AmaliTech training). A normalized (3NF)

hospital database is analysed for slow analytics queries, then

redesigned as a **star schema** with an ETL pipeline. The same four

business questions run **3.5x to 17.1x faster**, with identical answers.



## Results at a glance



| # | Business question | OLTP (3NF) | Star schema | Speed-up | Main reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Monthly encounters by specialty | 0.438 s | 0.125 s | 3.5x | specialty key on the fact, month from `dim_date` |
| Q2 | Top diagnosis-procedure pairs | 3.812 s | 0.672 s | 5.7x | narrow bridge tables, late name lookups |
| Q3 | 30-day readmission rate | 0.547 s | 0.032 s | 17.1x | readmission flag pre-computed in the ETL |
| Q4 | Revenue by specialty & month | 0.969 s | 0.094 s | 10.3x | revenue pre-aggregated on the fact |
| | **All four combined** | **5.766 s** | **0.923 s** | **6.2x** | |



Median of 3 warm runs on the same PC and the same data (~600k rows).

Full method and plans: [`docs/query_analysis.txt`](docs/query_analysis.txt)

and [`docs/star_schema_queries.txt`](docs/star_schema_queries.txt).

&#x20;  then reconnect (the data generator and ETL take longer than the 30 s default).

2. In the query toolbar, set the row limit to **Don't Limit**.

3. Open each script and run the **whole file** (nothing highlighted, `Ctrl + Shift + Enter`),

&#x20;  in this order:

## Design summary



### OLTP schema (Parts 1-2)

10 normalized tables (the lab text says 8). Bottlenecks found with `EXPLAIN ANALYZE`:

- **Join chains:** specialty is 2 hops from `encounters` and 3 from `billing`.

- **Row explosion:** joining two junction tables creates 373,894 pair rows (Q2).

- **Self-join:** readmissions need `encounters` joined to itself (Q3).

- **Functions on columns:** `DATE_FORMAT()` per row prevents index use.

- **Double-counting risk:** joining billing to diagnoses inflated revenue

&#x20; from $299.7M to $748.6M.



### Star schema (Part 3)



```
                 dim_date        dim_patient
                     │                │
 dim_encounter_type ─┼─ fact_encounters ─┼─ dim_provider
                     │       │        │
          dim_specialty      │      dim_department
                             │
   bridge_encounter_diagnoses ── dim_diagnosis
   bridge_encounter_procedures ── dim_procedure
```



- **Grain:** one row per encounter (money stored once, so it cannot be double-counted).

- **8 dimensions** with surrogate keys and an `Unknown` member (key 0)

&#x20; for missing data; `dim_date` uses a `YYYYMMDD` key and a `calendar_month` label.

- **Pre-aggregated measures on the fact:** diagnosis/procedure/claim counts,

&#x20; claim and allowed amounts, length of stay, and `readmitted_within_30d`.

- **Bridge tables** for diagnoses and procedures (many-to-many) keep the grain clean.

- **Covering indexes** built for each business question.



Details: [`docs/design_decisions.txt`](docs/design_decisions.txt).



### ETL (Part 3.4)

Full refresh in `06_etl_load.sql`: generate `dim_date` → Unknown members →

dimensions → fact (key lookups by natural key, child tables aggregated

**before** joining, readmission flag via `EXISTS`) → bridges →

`ANALYZE TABLE`. Production design (nightly incremental load, SCD Type 1

upserts, late-arriving claims and readmissions):

[`docs/etl_design.txt`](docs/etl_design.txt).



## Key findings



- The biggest gains come from **moving work into the ETL**: Q3 (17.1x)

&#x20; and Q4 (10.3x).

- Star queries do not always have fewer joins (Q1 has 3 vs 2), but every

&#x20; join is **one hop to a small dimension** on an integer key, and most

&#x20; plans use covering indexes only.

- Work that cannot be done in advance stays costly: unique-patient counts

&#x20; (Q1) and diagnosis x procedure pairing (Q2) improved least.

- Trade-offs: a second copy of the data (mostly indexes), more complex

&#x20; ETL, and data that is only as fresh as the last load.

&#x20; See [`docs/reflection.md`](docs/reflection.md).



## Business rules and assumptions



- **Readmission:** an Inpatient stay followed by another **Inpatient**

&#x20; admission of the same patient within 30 days of discharge

&#x20; (counting any return visit would give ~32-34% instead of ~5-7%).

- **Revenue:** `allowed_amount`, grouped by **service month** (encounter date);

&#x20; denied claims count as 0.

- **Specialty** of an encounter = specialty of its provider.

- Lab sample-data fix: `billing` is inserted after `encounters` (the lab's

&#x20; order fails with FK error 1452).



## Limitations



- Data is synthetic and evenly spread; real diagnosis and specialty

&#x20; distributions are more skewed.

- Orthopedics receives about twice the non-ER visits because of how the

&#x20; generator re-assigns visits away from Emergency doctors.

- Timings depend on the PC; the speed-up ratios are the meaningful result.

- The ETL is a tested full refresh; incremental loading is documented, not built.
